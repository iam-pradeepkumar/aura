The problem · India & Nepal

Recent disasters expose a deadly gap

South Asia faces earthquakes, landslides, and floods every year — yet when disaster strikes, responders still cannot answer three basic questions: where are the survivors, how many are there, and are they still alive?

Why rescue teams lose precious hours

  • No location — buried victims cannot call for help; rubble blocks GPS and phone signals.
  • No count — officials do not know how many people were in a building or village when it collapsed.
  • No right device — search dogs, microphones, and thermal cameras fail in mud, rain, and deep debris.
  • No local warning — national bulletins arrive too late; villages lack ward-level evacuation routes.

AURA closes this gap with affordable ESP32 sensing: early alerts before disaster, then WiFi CSI survivor detection after networks fail.

Phase 2 — Rescue sensing (WiFi CSI)

When cell towers fail, ESP32 nodes form a local mesh. WiFi probe packets bounce off human bodies; receivers measure CSI to find who, where, and vitals.

What CSI sensing detects

Channel State Information captures tiny changes in WiFi signals when a chest moves (breathing) or a limb shifts (motion). AURA fuses four receiver angles to estimate position inside a 10×10 m zone.

What the alert engine monitors

USGS global earthquake feed + Open-Meteo wind/precipitation for your watch coordinates. Thresholds in disaster_alert/config.yaml filter noise; only significant hazards enter the DM queue.
